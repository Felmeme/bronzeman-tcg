# Faux Cardcore transcript analysis

This planner profile was derived from the English caption tracks for Faux's ten edited
Cardcore episodes published through 28 July 2026. Timestamps link to the underlying
video. Statements are classified as either **rule** (a declared constraint or precedent)
or **strategy** (a route choice that depends on the current collection).

Auto-generated captions occasionally misspell OSRS names. The profile therefore uses
the captions for decisions and timestamps, while canonical item/NPC names come from the
OSRS TCG and Bronzeman TCG catalogues.

## Stable rules

1. **Item use is card-locked; acquisition is not.** Locked loot may be gathered and
   banked, but cannot be used or directly benefited from. [Ep. 2, 02:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VhZ3usP25gE&t=122s)
2. **Combat targets are card-locked.** Talking to NPCs is allowed, correcting the
   stricter idea floated in episode 1. A mandatory quest enemy with a card must be
   unlocked. [Ep. 2, 02:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VhZ3usP25gE&t=156s),
   [Ep. 5, 05:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ECW6yWbtIE&t=359s)
3. **No catalogue entry means no restriction.** This applies to items such as the
   bearhead and to non-card NPCs, but the catalogue must be checked first.
   [Ep. 9, 23:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9gkCEhDM3A&t=1385s)
4. **Cards unlock permission, not possession.** The HCIM must still obtain an unlocked
   item through a legal spawn, monster, shop or production chain.
   [Ep. 1, 05:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q1l89TiTQ8s&t=358s)
5. **Quest hand-ins are item use.** Required items need cards, though OSRS-supported
   substitutes are valid (for example, plank/log or jug/bucket variants where the quest
   accepts either). [Ep. 10, 07:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0w8Lh0GOBD4&t=424s),
   [Ep. 9, 08:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9gkCEhDM3A&t=523s)
6. **Only duplicate cards are sold for credits.** Valuable but currently useless unique
   cards stay in the collection. [Ep. 5, 07:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ECW6yWbtIE&t=454s)
7. **Foils unlock downward within their equipment category.** A foil unlocks every
   lower-tier item in the same category. A top-tier foil covers the entire slot beneath
   it; for example, foil Torva full helm unlocks every helmet.
   [Ep. 2, 06:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VhZ3usP25gE&t=393s),
   [Ep. 3, 06:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fk0YvYa8fVY&t=369s)
8. **Foil bosses unlock their collection-log uniques; foil pets unlock the pet family.**
   A foil quest boss does not grant unrelated quest ingredients.
   [Ep. 2, 07:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VhZ3usP25gE&t=450s),
   [Ep. 9, 26:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9gkCEhDM3A&t=1574s),
   [Ep. 10, 08:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0w8Lh0GOBD4&t=500s)
9. **Ambiguity resolves conservatively.** When no precedent exists, Faux explicitly
   chooses the harder reasonable route. [Ep. 10, 08:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0w8Lh0GOBD4&t=524s)
10. **Material violations receive proportional repayment.** After using a locked cave
    lizard to help obtain a rune dagger, he earned and sacrificed another dagger before
    using the reward. [Ep. 9, 04:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9gkCEhDM3A&t=275s)
11. **Mystic Cards are a cosmetic whitelist.** Faux permits this identity item because
    it gives no gameplay benefit. [Ep. 4, 01:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=afBgcgy8n-A&t=69s)

## Strategy extracted by episode

- **Episode 1:** Establishes Fire Cape as the first finish line and uses safe Draynor
  Agility to bootstrap the first 2,500-credit packs. Early common cards are valued over
  inaccessible high-rarity equipment. [03:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q1l89TiTQ8s&t=190s)
- **Episode 2:** Formalizes the stable rules. Free minigame travel substitutes for coins;
  a small fishing net, food and a low-level monster card are treated as breakthrough
  unlocks because they open skills and kill credits.
- **Episode 3:** A foil rune axe demonstrates the item-family rule and creates a complete
  early Woodcutting route. The account obtains the best legal axe from a world spawn
  after unlocking it. [05:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fk0YvYa8fVY&t=327s)
- **Episode 4:** Whitelists Mystic Cards, treats clues as available when the clue itself
  has no card restriction, and searches spawns/drop tables immediately after each useful
  pull. [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=afBgcgy8n-A)
- **Episode 5:** Explicitly ranks skill-opening cards as the most important class of pull.
  Uses another Cardcore player for Shield of Arrav only after verifying that each player
  owns their side's required unlocks. [06:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ECW6yWbtIE&t=401s),
  [11:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ECW6yWbtIE&t=681s)
- **Episode 6:** Prioritizes Children of the Sun/Quetzal travel and The Grand Tree/gliders
  because transport changes every later route. Also warns that readiness plugins are
  helpful approximations rather than authoritative rulings.
  [00:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KLYnVnnL-50&t=9s),
  [03:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KLYnVnnL-50&t=211s)
- **Episode 7:** Uses the highest legal XP/hour method as a credit engine because every
  1,000 non-combat XP yields credits, then inspects food-component chains instead of
  treating a finished-food card as immediately obtainable.
  [03:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7TZ5_Xfe88&t=181s)
- **Episode 8:** Demonstrates acquisition-path analysis: stats alone cannot solve a gear
  bottleneck, so target legal monsters, spawns and quests that provide the required
  weapon. It also identifies Wintertodt as a later high-credit method.
  [09:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h0mFLnsh92Y&t=566s),
  [16:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h0mFLnsh92Y&t=981s)
- **Episode 9:** Adds the proportional penalty precedent, exploits quest-supported item
  substitutes, and prioritizes quest XP/quest points. It also blocks MTA until the
  progress-hat card is owned despite the item no longer needing to be carried.
- **Episode 10:** Ranks a short list of account-changing pulls: combat runes, high-value
  NPCs, skill tools, farming tools and quest talismans. It confirms that collection
  percentage is a poor proxy for progression value. [02:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0w8Lh0GOBD4&t=138s)

## Planner implications

The recommendation score should favor, in order:

1. A legal and obtainable unlock that opens a whole skill or transport network.
2. A Fire Cape or Recipe-for-Disaster dependency.
3. A legal high-XP/high-kill-credit activity.
4. A quest that grants transport, large XP, quest points or a new acquisition source.
5. A multi-benefit monster whose drop table supplies several already-owned cards.
6. Banking future-value outputs when the next production stage remains locked.

The planner must show **card owned** and **item obtainable now** as different states.
That distinction is the most repeated strategic idea across the series.
